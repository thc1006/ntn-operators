# Cross-validation: NTNSlice constellation-pool availability & HA failover-backoff persistence

- Date: 2026-07-30
- Method: multi-agent deep research (5 search angles → 19 sources fetched → 80 claims extracted → 25 adversarially verified by 3-vote panels; 23 confirmed, 2 refuted). Both conclusions **VALIDATED**.
- Purpose: independently cross-validate two design conclusions reached by review, before enshrining them (ADR-0008 for #1; keep-as-is for #2 / PR #270).

## Conclusion 1 — Satellite-path availability is CONSTELLATION-POOL-level, not NORAD-pinned → **VALIDATED**

**Claim:** `NTNSlice` should report satellite-path availability as "is ANY tracked constellation member currently overhead AND deliverable (fresh source epoch)", NOT pinned to one satellite's NORAD.

**Evidence (for):**
- **3GPP TR 38.821 (normative study, verbatim):** "It is necessary to have a constellation of several Non-Geostationary satellites associated with handover mechanisms to ensure a service continuity." §7.3.2.1.4: non-GEO satellites "move with high speed relative to a fixed position on earth, leading to frequent and unavoidable handover." §8.3 enumerates **Inter-satellite hand-over** (connection moved between DIFFERENT satellites), mapped to the Inter-gNB / Inter-gNB-CU handover procedure — so a UE's connection is handed across constellation members, not pinned to one.
- **Rel-17/18 enhancements strengthen the pool model:** Rel-17 Conditional Handover adds a time/location-triggered "satellite switch"; Rel-18 satellite-switch-with-re-sync keeps the SAME PCI while changing serving satellite — formally decoupling cell identity from the physical satellite.
- **A region is covered by MANY simultaneous candidates:** a Rel-17 quasi-earth-fixed cell "can be served by several satellites, which will create multiple targeted satellite options in the handover process"; 51–77 (measured) / 30–50 (IETF 118) LEO satellites are simultaneously visible to a fixed receiver.
- **Real systems confirm handover-based continuity:** Starlink reassigns a terminal on a 15-second globally-synchronized grid, each satellite usable ~3 min (IETF 118 IEPG; replicated by arXiv 2310.09242, 2307.00402); OneWeb terminals "maintain a continuous link as the satellites move across the sky, using handovers between satellites and beams"; Iridium hands off "like cellular systems such as GSM."

**Nuance (the one mode where per-satellite matters — does NOT refute):** a bent-pipe transparent-payload pass with earth-moving cells ties the *physical link* to one satellite FOR THAT PASS — so `NTNCellConfig`'s per-NORAD SIB19 selection is correct **at the cell/broadcast layer**. But the next pass is a different member, so **slice-level** continuous availability remains a pool property. The two-layer split — pool-level slice availability + per-NORAD cell config — is the right architecture (TR 38.821 lists inter-satellite handover for the transparent column too, refuting "bent-pipe ⇒ single-satellite sessions").

**Design implication:** keep pool-level `NTNSlice` availability (as implemented, per-member freshness-gated by #275); do NOT bind slice availability to a single NORAD. Documented in **ADR-0008**.

**Sources:** [3GPP TR 38.821 (ATIS mirror)](https://www.atis.org/wp-content/uploads/3gpp-documents/Rel16/ATIS.3GPP.38.821.V1600.pdf) · [IEEE Network 2024, arXiv 2405.06801](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06801v1) · [MDPI Sensors 2023, PMC10301820](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10301820/) · [Globecom Wksp 2022, arXiv 2211.07872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.07872) · [Ericsson "5G from Space", arXiv 2103.09156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.09156) · [IETF 118 Starlink (IEPG)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/ietf-118-starlink-protocol-performance/263957165) · [Starlink perf, arXiv 2310.09242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09242) · [OneWeb (eoportal)](https://www.eoportal.org/satellite-missions/oneweb) · [Iridium (Microwave Journal)](https://www.microwavejournal.com/articles/37587-the-iridium-leo-satellite-system-for-global-mobile-communications)

## Conclusion 2 — Don't persist fetch retry/backoff state across failover → **VALIDATED**

**Claim:** persisting last-good DATA (OMM) to a ConfigMap while letting a fresh leader reset backoff and make ONE polite probe on takeover is correct; persisting a wall-clock next-attempt DEADLINE across pods is an anti-pattern.

**Evidence (for):**
- **Go anti-pattern (primary, pkg.go.dev/time):** "Because the monotonic clock reading has no meaning outside the current process, the serialized forms generated by GobEncode, MarshalBinary, MarshalJSON, MarshalText omit the monotonic clock reading." Deserializers/constructors "always create times with no monotonic clock reading," and After/Before/Sub "fall back to using the wall clock readings" — which is not stable across pods and is NTP-correctable. So a serialized next-attempt deadline is unreliable across a failover.
- **CelesTrak politeness (primary, usage-policy):** "For GP data, updates are once every 2 hours"; enforcement targets abuse ("immediately stop querying when it receives any non-HTTP 200 responses… Repeatedly ignoring them will… send your IP address to the firewall"). A single takeover probe that honors non-200 is squarely within policy; a hot-retry loop is the firewall trigger.
- **Recovery benefit:** a fresh leader that resets backoff detects upstream recovery faster than one inheriting a stale (up to 15 min / 2–24 h) backoff.

**Refinement (already satisfied):** the research suggested freshness-gating the takeover probe against the persisted last-fetch so clustered failovers don't duplicate a fetch inside one 2-h GP cycle. **PR #270 already does this**: `restoreOMMCache` restores the ORIGINAL `FetchedAt` from the ConfigMap annotation, so `cacheServe` window-serves (no fetch) when the last fetch is still within the refresh window — a cluster of failovers inside one window does not re-fetch. No change needed.

**Design implication:** keep #270 as-is (persist elements, not retry timers). The #273 marker fix independently confirms the wall-clock-across-processes hazard in this codebase.

## Transparency

- **2 of 25 claims were REFUTED** (killed at 1-2 votes) and excluded: an over-strong handover-motivation claim (arXiv 2103.09156) and an over-literal "15s = new satellite every slot" claim (arXiv 2405.06801). Their underlying facts survive via other confirmed claims.
- **Caveats:** TR 38.821 is a Rel-16 STUDY; the normative procedures are in TS 38.300/38.331/38.401 (under-citation, not overreach). The 6.61–132.38 s figures are earth-MOVING cell-dwell times, not single-satellite visibility (they understate single-satellite service, only strengthening the pool conclusion). Starlink's 15 s is a reassignment *opportunity*, not a guaranteed new satellite per slot.
